# 6. Invariant versus equivariant geometry networks

## 6.1 Decision

Exact symmetry compliance does **not** require learned \(SO(3)\)-vector hidden
channels provided that the continuous model acts on the already constructed
metric--Wyckoff quotient and uses a certified covariant output construction.
This is an architectural-admissibility statement, not evidence that such hidden
channels are unnecessary for finite-model accuracy, optimization, or data
efficiency.  The implemented primary candidate is therefore a
dense, scalar, periodic quotient Transformer whose invariant coefficients
multiply deterministic covariant bases before the existing exact
Cartesian-to-intrinsic adjoint.  It predicts \(v_z\) directly and obtains
\(v_\eta\) through that adjoint.  This candidate removes cutoff graphs,
neighbour lists, sparse source--destination edges, and scatter-based graph
message passing from the learned geometry network.

This is not a claim that equivariance is useless or that the scalar-hidden
candidate is more accurate.  The current equivariant network remains an
ablation candidate for the future matched experiment in section 6.8.  Nor is the proposed model
"message-passing-free" in the broad mathematical sense: self-attention still
aggregates information from other tokens.  The precise claim is that it removes
**sparse local graph message passing** while retaining dense global attention.

AlphaFold 3 is relevant empirical evidence, but it is often described
incorrectly.  Its diffusion module is **non-equivariant**, not an exactly
invariant geometric network: it acts on raw coordinates, omits rotational
frames and explicit equivariant processing, and uses random rotation and
translation.  Its Pairformer and diffusion Transformer still communicate
through pair representations and attention.  AlphaFold 3 therefore shows that
explicit equivariant layers are not always necessary for high accuracy on its
biomolecular task; it does not prove the same conclusion for periodic crystal
generation.

## 6.2 The exact obstruction for a Cartesian output

Let \(E(X)\) be a strictly rotation-invariant scalar encoding,

\[
E(XQ)=E(X),\qquad Q\in SO(3),
\tag{6.1}
\]

and let a scalar-only head attempt to return a Cartesian polar vector
\(F(X)=H(E(X))\in\mathbb R^3\).  Invariance gives
\(F(XQ)=F(X)\), whereas physical covariance requires

\[
F(XQ)=F(X)Q .
\tag{6.2}
\]

Consequently \(F(X)=F(X)Q\) for every \(Q\in SO(3)\), whose only solution is
\(F(X)=0\).  Thus a strictly invariant representation cannot directly emit a
nonzero Cartesian vector while guaranteeing rotation covariance.

This does **not** imply that learned vector channels are necessary.  A scalar
network may predict invariant coefficients multiplying deterministic covariant
bases, for example

\[
F_i(X)
=
\sum_j a_{ij}(I(X))d_{ij}
+
\sum_{j,k}b_{ijk}(I(X))\bigl(d_{ij}\times d_{ik}\bigr),
\tag{6.3}
\]

where \(I(X)\) contains dot products and proper-rotation invariants.  Equation
(6.3) is \(SO(3)\)-equivariant although all learned coefficients are scalars.
This is the relevant consequence of the invariant-theory results in
[Scalars are Universal](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2021/hash/f1b0775946bc0329b35b823b86eeb5f5-Abstract.html):
for orthogonal-group covariance, vector outputs are generated by invariant
scalar coefficients and input vectors; for proper rotations, oriented products
must also be available.  The result is an expressivity theorem, not a claim
about optimization, data efficiency, or finite-network superiority.

## 6.3 Why the obstruction does not apply to the CrystalAtlas state

Use the row-vector convention \(x=fL\), with \(L\in GL^+(3)\).  An external
proper rotation acts as

\[
(L,f)\longmapsto(LQ,f),\qquad Q\in SO(3),
\tag{6.4}
\]

and leaves the metric unchanged:

\[
G=LL^{\mathsf T},\qquad
(LQ)(LQ)^{\mathsf T}=G.
\tag{6.5}
\]

CrystalAtlas stores

\[
q=(z,\eta),\qquad G=G_g(\kappa_g(z)),
\tag{6.6}
\]

where \(z\) is the bounded invariant metric coordinate and \(\eta\) contains
origin-reduced intrinsic Wyckoff coordinates in the pinned Hall setting.  The
decoder chooses the right-handed section \(L=L(G)\); a free external rotation
is not part of \(q\).  Translation is separately quotiented.  Therefore

\[
q(LQ,f)=q(L,f),
\qquad
v_q=(v_z,v_\eta)\in T_q\mathcal Q_\alpha
\tag{6.7}
\]

has no Cartesian \(SO(3)\) index.  A scalar Transformer may predict \(v_q\)
directly without contradicting (6.1)--(6.2).  The implemented model instead
retains the exact expand--covariant-readout--reduce interface because it handles
every cover through one shared atom-set map and provides a direct legal-tangent
certificate.  Both choices avoid learned equivariant hidden channels.

This conclusion depends on the quotient actually being complete.  A pinned
Hall setting and a right-handed lattice do not by themselves remove repeated
orbit permutations, finite origin choices, affine-normalizer representatives,
or other residual chart actions.  For every retained
\(\gamma\in\Gamma_\alpha^{\rm res}\), the intrinsic field must satisfy

\[
v(t,\gamma q,\alpha,c)
=d\gamma_q\,v(t,q,\alpha,c).
\tag{6.8}
\]

When a unique section is not proved, enforce (6.8) exactly by

\[
\bar v(q)
=
\frac{1}{|\Gamma_\alpha^{\rm res}|}
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_\alpha^{\rm res}}
d\gamma^{-1}_{\gamma q}\,v(\gamma q).
\tag{6.9}
\]

Repeated identical orbit tokens must remain permutation-equivariant.  Hence
removing learned \(SO(3)\)-representation-valued hidden channels and quotienting
the external orientation do not remove crystallographic gauge obligations.

## 6.4 Dense periodic quotient attention

The model may deterministically expand the intrinsic state to fractional
positions only to construct scalar geometry features.  Let
\(\Delta f_{ij}=f_j-f_i\), let \(L=L(G)\), and choose a finite reciprocal set
\(\mathcal K_g\subset\mathbb Z^3\) that is closed under \(k\mapsto-k\).  The
implementation uses the complete cube \([-K,K]^3\) for heat sums and
reciprocal covariants, including the zero mode in the normalized heat channels.
The raw learned pair projection stores only one representative of each exactly
redundant pair \(\{k,-k\}\), because their cosine columns are equal and their
sine columns differ only by sign; the zero sine is omitted.  This is exact
linear compression, not a spectral truncation.  The pinned basis is not changed inside one
field evaluation; every currently retained within-cover action expands to atom
permutations plus integer image relabellings.  Any future retained action that
changes the fractional basis must also preserve \(\mathcal K_g\), or the mode
set must be enlarged to its finite orbit before that action is admitted.  Define

\[
\widehat r_k=2\pi\ell_0 kL^{-\mathsf T},
\qquad
\theta_{ijk}=2\pi k\cdot\Delta f_{ij},
\tag{6.10}
\]

For the implemented heat weights define

\[
w_p(s)=e^{-\tau_p s},\qquad
Z_p(L)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_g}w_p(\lVert\widehat r_k\rVert^2).
\]

The zero reciprocal mode implies (Z_p(L)\geq1).  The smooth normalized
periodic features implemented in code are

\[
C_{ij}^{p}
=\frac{1}{Z_p(L)}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_g}
w_p(\lVert \widehat r_k\rVert^2)\cos\theta_{ijk},
\qquad
D_{ij}^{p}
=\frac{1}{Z_p(L)}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_g}
w_p(\lVert \widehat r_k\rVert^2)\sin\theta_{ijk}\,\widehat r_k.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Integer image shifts of either atom leave every phase unchanged.  Under
an external rotation \(L\mapsto LQ\), \(Q\in SO(3)\), \(C_{ij}^{p}\) is unchanged and
\(D_{ij}^{p}\mapsto D_{ij}^{p}Q\).  The factor \(\ell_0\) makes the reciprocal
carriers dimensionless, just as dividing direct carriers by \(\ell_0\) does,
so a consistent change of length units leaves them unchanged.  No
minimum-image choice or cutoff graph is present.  The implemented learned pair
path receives one independent representative of each raw sine/cosine phase,
the normalized heat scalars \(C\), and the oriented scalar features below;
Cartesian covariants are reserved for the deterministic readout.

For harmonics \(h=1,\ldots,H\), the implemented-default direct carriers and
pinned-frame carriers are

\[
A_{ij}^{h}
=u_\epsilon\!\left[
\frac{\sin(2\pi h\,\Delta f_{ij})}{h}\frac{L}{\ell_0}
\right],
\qquad
E_a=u_\epsilon\!\left(\frac{L_{a:}}{\ell_0}\right),
\quad a=1,2,3.
\tag{6.11a}
\]

Here the sine is applied componentwise to the fractional row vector.
The three \(E_a\) are broadcast to every ordered atom pair.  They are rows of
the pinned right-handed lattice section, not a learned alignment.  They retain
nonzero polar direction carriers when all sine-odd direct and reciprocal
moments vanish at a complete advertised-slot collision.

Every deterministic vector carrier is normalized with
\(u_\epsilon(v)=v/\sqrt{\lVert v\rVert^2+\epsilon}\), not \(v/\lVert v\rVert\).
Hence collisions remain smooth and
\(\lVert D u_\epsilon\rVert_2\le1/\sqrt\epsilon\).  The implemented
dimensionless default \(\epsilon=10^{-4}\) caps this preprocessing Jacobian at
100; it is a solver-stiffness hyperparameter and must be included in the NFE
ablation rather than treated as an invisible numerical constant.

A scalar-only distance or cosine representation is accidentally \(O(3)\)
invariant and cannot distinguish enantiomers.  The implementation normalizes a
fixed ordered family of primary direct and reciprocal pair covariants
\(P^{\rm prim}_{ijb}\),
averages the first three distinct primary families over source atoms, and forms

\[
M_{ib}=\frac1N\sum_j P^{\rm prim}_{ijb},
\qquad
\tau_i=\tanh\!\left[M_{i1}\cdot(M_{i2}\times M_{i3})\right].
\tag{6.12}
\]

The signed triple \(\tau_i\) is invariant under \(SO(3)\) and changes sign under
a reflection whenever it is nonzero.  It must not be replaced by an absolute
value or averaged over improper external transformations because the physical
quotient identifies proper rigid motions, not enantiomers.  This makes a tested
mirror witness distinguishable but does not prove injectivity for every
enantiomeric pair.  The pinned decoder still enforces any
improper operations that belong to the crystal's own space group.  Learned
species-dependent moment coefficients are a potentially more expressive
ablation; they are not part of the current implementation and must not be
inferred from (6.12).

With scalar token states \(h_i\) and scalar pair data \(P_{ij}\) constructed
from (6.11)--(6.12), use full within-crystal attention

\[
a_{ij}^{(h)}
=
\operatorname{softmax}_{j}
\left[
\frac{q_h(h_i)^{\mathsf T}k_h(h_j)}{\sqrt{d_h}}
+b_h(P_{ij})
\right],
\qquad
h_i'
=h_i+W_o\!\bigoplus_h\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}^{(h)}
\left[v_h(h_j)+p_h(P_{ij})\right].
\tag{6.13}
\]

Only the cross-crystal batch mask is sparse.  Pooled scalar channels return
\(v_z\in\mathbb R^{d_g}\).  Let \(B_{ijb}\) collect the normalized dimensionless
direct moments \(A\), reciprocal heat-gradient moments \(D\), their
direct--reciprocal cross products, and the three pinned-frame carriers \(E\).
The implemented readout predicts invariant coefficients and forms

\[
\widehat s_{ic}
=\frac1N\sum_{j,b}a_{ijcb}(h_i,h_j,P_{ij})B_{ijb},
\tag{6.13a}
\]

then obtains \(v_\eta\) with the exact metric adjoint in equations
(3.9)--(3.10).  Learned hidden channels are scalar and the expanded Cartesian
output is \(SO(3)\)-covariant.  The reduced intrinsic field has no Cartesian
rotation index and is exactly tangent to the selected cover.

Equation (6.13) is nevertheless information aggregation.  A graph
message-passing layer and dense attention have the respective forms

\[
h_i'=U\!\left(h_i,\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}M(h_i,h_j,e_{ij})\right),
\qquad
h_i'=U\!\left(h_i,\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}V(h_j)\right).
\tag{6.14}
\]

The second is dynamic message passing on a complete graph if "message
passing" is used as a mathematical umbrella term.  What disappears is local
neighbour construction, cutoff topology, edge scatter, and the need for many
layers merely to propagate information beyond a cutoff.

Finite Fourier features are not automatically injective, and dense attention
costs \(O(N^2d)\) memory and work.  Reciprocal-mode completeness, aliasing,
long-range accuracy, and scaling over the advertised atom domain are proof and
benchmark obligations, not consequences of (6.10)--(6.13a).

## 6.5 Direct-intrinsic alternative

Because \(v_\eta\) has no Cartesian index, a future alternative may pool
orbit-aware scalar tokens and predict one intrinsic velocity per advertised
orbit directly.  It would remove the Cartesian readout and adjoint from the
neural path.  Such a head is valid only if its orbit correspondence, repeated-
orbit permutation action, continuous-origin quotient, and every residual chart
action are encoded exactly.  The present expanded-atom interface does not yet
provide that certified orbit-token map, so this remains an ablation rather than
the default.

A more expressive future covariant construction could instead be written

\[
\widehat s_i
=
\sum_a\alpha_{ia}(h)M_{ia}
+\sum_{a<b}\beta_{iab}(h)(M_{ia}\times M_{ib}),
\tag{6.15}
\]

followed by the exact metric adjoint already specified for \(v_\eta\).  This is
a learned-moment ablation, not an algebraic restatement of the current same-pair
basis contraction (6.13a), because it contains cross-neighbour moment products
that contraction does not.  In both cases learned hidden channels and
coefficients remain scalar while the expanded Cartesian map is
\(SO(3)\)-covariant and its intrinsic reduction is rotation-invariant.
This is the correct interpretation of a "scalar-invariant Transformer that
outputs geometry"; an invariant Cartesian vector head with no covariant basis would be
mathematically invalid by section 6.2.

The implemented direct, reciprocal, and pinned lattice-frame carriers are polar
under reflections, whereas the direct--reciprocal cross products are axial.
Ordinary \(SO(3)\)-scalar coefficients
may mix them, so the output has no declared \(O(3)\) parity: it is an
\(SO(3)\) vector, not necessarily a polar vector under an improper transform.
Not quotienting reflections keeps enantiomers in distinct physical equivalence
classes.  A learned representation additionally needs reflection-odd or
parity-typed information if it is to assign them different values; the current
signed feature permits that distinction but does not guarantee it for every
pair.  None of this proves that the physical field should break parity.  An
\(O(3)\)-equivariant parity-typed readout is therefore a required matched
ablation, particularly for parity-symmetric nonrelativistic materials data.

## 6.6 What AlphaFold 3 does and does not establish

The [AlphaFold 3 Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07487-w)
and its
[Supplementary Methods](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-024-07487-w/MediaObjects/41586_2024_7487_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
state that the diffusion module operates on raw atom coordinates without
rotational frames or equivariant processing.  The architecture uses random
rotation and translation, a dense \(n\times n\) pair representation, Pairformer
updates, attention with pair bias, and atom/token attention.  It therefore
removes explicit geometric equivariance but emphatically does not remove
pairwise communication or attention aggregation.

AF3 is not a controlled comparison against an equivariant crystal generator.
Its conditioning, scale, data, molecular connectivity, nonperiodic geometry,
loss, and evaluation task all differ from CrystalAtlas.  Its success is
evidence that a non-equivariant Transformer can learn a demanding 3D mapping;
it is not a proof that augmentation is preferable to exact symmetry, that its
architecture transfers to crystals, or that sparse geometric inductive bias is
obsolete.

Learned rotational alignment is another viable empirical route.  The ICML
paper
[Scalable Non-Equivariant 3D Molecule Generation via Rotational Alignment](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/ding25a.html)
reports competitive molecular generation after learning a sample-dependent
\(SO(3)\) alignment.  That is evidence for finite molecules, not an exact
periodic-crystal theorem.  Canonical frames can be nonunique or unstable at
symmetric configurations.  CrystalAtlas should not learn a second alignment:
its invariant metric and pinned right-handed Wyckoff chart already remove the
external rotation, subject to the residual-gauge certificates in (6.8)--(6.9).

## 6.7 Prior evidence and its limits

| Primary source | Evidence it supplies | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| [Yun et al., ICLR 2020](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ByxRM0Ntvr) | Transformers can universally approximate continuous permutation-equivariant sequence maps on a compact domain under the paper's assumptions. | Trainability, sample efficiency, periodic correctness, variable-size universality, or superiority to geometric networks. |
| [Scalars are Universal, NeurIPS 2021](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2021/hash/f1b0775946bc0329b35b823b86eeb5f5-Abstract.html) | Scalar invariants plus deterministic vector/cross-product bases can parameterize broad equivariant function classes. | That distance-only features preserve chirality, or that finite scalar models outperform irreducible-representation networks. |
| [CrystalFormer: space-group-informed generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15734) | An ordinary autoregressive Transformer can model symmetry-reduced Wyckoff/site variables for crystal generation. | Exact coverage of this package's quotient, continuous-flow quality, or removal of all gauge ambiguity. |
| [Crystalformer: infinitely connected attention, ICLR 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=fxQiecl9HB) | Periodic all-image attention can be formulated tractably and perform strongly as a crystal property encoder. | Generative superiority or the completeness of the finite Fourier construction in (6.11). |
| [CrystalFlow](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64364-4) | Conditional flow matching with a graph-based equivariant network is effective for crystal generation. | Necessity of an equivariant GNN, or superiority over a matched dense quotient Transformer. |
| [CrystalDiT, AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37121) | A unified diffusion Transformer is a serious non-GNN crystal-generation baseline. | Exact symmetry/gauge guarantees or transfer of its reported ranking under CrystalAtlas data and evaluation. |
| [MatterGen, Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08628-5) | A large equivariant graph score network can generate and condition inorganic materials effectively. | That graph equivariance is necessary, or that its result is architecture-only rather than data/training/system scale. |
| [Rotational alignment, ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/ding25a.html) | A learned alignment can make non-equivariant molecular diffusion competitive and more efficient in the reported setting. | A continuous, unique, exact all-crystal gauge or a result on periodic inorganic generation. |

The universal-approximation and invariant-theory rows are mathematical
expressivity results under stated assumptions.  The remaining rows are
empirical precedents on different representations, datasets, and tasks.  None
establishes predictive superiority for the proposed CrystalAtlas replacement.

## 6.8 Falsifiable matched ablation

Compare four models while freezing the composition model, scaffold selector,
catalogue, training records and splits, flow path/base law, loss metric,
optimizer, batch atom budget, solver, number of function evaluations, and
candidate ranking protocol:

1. the present sparse periodic \(SE(3)\)-equivariant graph Transformer;
2. the implemented dense scalar quotient Transformer with the covariant
   readout (6.13a) and exact Cartesian reduction;
3. the same dense scalar Transformer with certified direct intrinsic heads
   from section 6.5, once that orbit-token contract exists; and
4. an AF3-style raw-coordinate non-equivariant Transformer with rotation and
   translation augmentation, only as a diagnostic baseline.

Run both parameter-matched and measured-compute-matched tracks.  Match the
hyperparameter-search budget, use at least five paired seeds, and report
confidence intervals rather than the best seed.  Before quality comparison,
every candidate must pass hard tests for atom permutation, integer-cell
relabelling, origin handling, all catalogue residual actions, exact decoded
space-group containment, collision limits, and mirror-related chiral P1 pairs.
For the raw-coordinate baseline measure

\[
\epsilon_{\rm rot}
=
\mathbb E_{X,Q}
\frac{\|F(XQ)-F(X)Q\|}
{\|F(X)\|+\varepsilon};
\tag{6.16}
\]

for quotient models measure the stronger chart identity \(q(XQ)=q(X)\) and
the residual-gauge defect

\[
\epsilon_\Gamma
=
\max_{\gamma\in\Gamma_\alpha^{\rm res}}
\frac{\|v(\gamma q)-d\gamma_qv(q)\|}
{\|v(q)\|+\varepsilon}.
\tag{6.17}
\]

Quality endpoints are tiny-set exact overfit, oracle-scaffold geometry
Match@\(K\), predicted-scaffold end-to-end Match@\(K\), composition-stratified
validity/uniqueness/novelty, post-relaxation energy-above-hull and SUN under the
same relaxer, chirality recall, wall time, peak memory, and quality versus NFE.

Adopt the dense quotient model for a large predictive run only if it passes
every exact gate and improves the preregistered quality--compute Pareto
frontier.  If it is faster but less
data-efficient, or if the equivariant model is materially better on held-out
chemistry/prototypes, retain the equivariant model.  Until this experiment is
run, the scientifically defensible conclusion is only **symmetry-compatible
architectural admissibility**, not predictive superiority.

## 6.9 Implementation-efficiency evidence boundary

An exploratory local CPU check motivated packaging a reproducible backend
comparison, but that exploratory run has no committed content-addressed report,
exact command, revision, or runtime manifest.  Its numerical timings therefore
are **not package evidence** and no speed ratio from it should be cited.

The packaged `benchmark-backends` command now records a deterministic
constant-density same-workload comparison with separate dense-pair and periodic-
image-edge semantics, forward and training-step timings, parameters, gradients,
logical memory, and available allocator telemetry.  It is still not parameter-,
weight-, FLOP-, or measured-compute-matched, and a committed CPU report would
not establish GPU behavior, multi-device scaling, predictive quality, or sample
quality.  Only archived reports from the preregistered study in section 6.8 can
satisfy the adoption rule or justify removing the equivariant ablation.
